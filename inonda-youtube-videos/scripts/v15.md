## Section 2: Water, roofs and damp (1:14-2:36)
> Start with water, because water causes most of the expensive problems.
>
> Look at the roof or terrace. Is the waterproofing cracked, bubbled or patched? Does water pool in one area? Check the ceilings below for stains, especially in corners and around windows. Stains can be old or new, so ask.
>
> Look for damp. Peeling paint, bubbling plaster, dark spots, a musty smell, or furniture pushed away from walls. A freshly painted patch on one wall can be innocent, and it can also be covering something. Ask why.
>
> Check the bathrooms and kitchen. Open taps and see the pressure. Flush toilets. Look under sinks. Check around the shower for loose tiles, failing silicone and soft spots.
>
> Ask about the water supply and tank. Where is the water stored, how old is the tank, and is there a pump? Check the water heater, and ask how old it is. Replacing one costs [ADD REAL PRICE RANGE, e.g. EUR X-Y], so it matters for your budget.
>
> Roofing and waterproofing, damp treatment and plumbing repairs are all part of our work, so we know how quickly these costs can add up.

## Section 3: Structure and exterior (2:36-3:31)
> Now the structure. Walk around the outside. Look for cracks. Many hairline cracks are cosmetic. But wide cracks, stepped cracks, or cracks that run across a wall and through the corners of doors and windows should be looked at by a structural professional. Do not guess.
>
> Look at the ground around the property. Does the land slope towards the house? Where does the rainwater go? Are the drains clear? Check boundary walls, gates, steps and balconies for rust, loose rails and crumbling concrete. Rusted reinforcement showing through concrete is a warning sign.
>
> Check the windows and doors. Do they open and close properly? Are the frames in good condition? Are the seals intact? Poor windows can mean heat, noise and rain getting in.

## Section 4: Electrics, cooling and extras (3:49-5:00)
> Electrics. Look at the switchboard. Is it tidy and labelled, or a tangle? Test the sockets and lights. Look for scorch marks, exposed wires, or mismatched, patched-in work. You cannot judge wiring behind walls from a viewing. So ask for any electrical documentation, and have a qualified electrician inspect before you commit.
>
> Air conditioning and heating. Ask how old the units are and when they were last serviced. Turn them on. Do they cool? Do they make noise or drip?
>
> If there is a pool, ask how old the equipment is, look at the pump and filter room, and look at the surface. Pools are lovely, and they cost money to run. Ask what the yearly running cost is, and treat any number as a guide: [ADD REAL RANGE, e.g. EUR X-Y per year].
>
> Also check the basics are connected. Water, electricity, drainage or septic, internet. A property may look finished but not be connected yet, and that can take time.
