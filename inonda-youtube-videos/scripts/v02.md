## Step 1: The walkthrough and the priority list (1:05-1:55)
> First, walk through every room with a notebook, and sort the problems into three groups. Group one: safety and structure. Damp, leaks, faulty wiring, dangerous balconies, anything a tenant could be hurt by. Group two: things a tenant will notice immediately, like a tired kitchen, a poor bathroom, bad lighting, and old windows that do not close properly. Group three: cosmetic extras. Fix group one first, always. Do group two next. Group three is where you can trim the budget. This is exactly how we walk a property with an owner, and it keeps the plan clear and the quote honest. A rental does not need designer finishes. It needs to be clean, dry, safe, and durable.

**On-screen text:** "1. Priority list" / "Safety, then tenant-visible, then cosmetic".

## Step 2: Damp, leaks and the hidden problems (1:55-2:45)
> Step two is dealing with damp, leaks and anything hidden. Painting over damp is the classic mistake. It looks fine for a few weeks, then the stain returns. You have to find the source first. It could be a roof or terrace leak, a plumbing fault, condensation from poor ventilation, or moisture rising in a wall. Each has a different fix. Once the source is solved, the wall can be treated, dried and repaired properly. Damp treatment is a core part of our work, and we always start with the cause, never the stain. Ask your contractor to explain the cause in plain language. If they cannot, get a second opinion.

## Step 3: Electrics, plumbing and cooling (3:05-3:50)
> Step three is the systems. Wiring in old apartments can be outdated, so have a qualified electrician inspect it and test it, and replace whatever is unsafe. Check the plumbing for slow drains, weak pressure and old taps. And think about comfort. Tenants in Cyprus care about summer heat and winter cold, so working air conditioning, decent insulation where possible, and windows and shutters that actually seal make a property more attractive. Do check what your building allows before installing outdoor units, because some buildings have rules. Local knowledge helps here, and it is something we bring to every property we look at.

## Step 4: Floors, walls and the kitchen and bathroom (3:50-4:40)
> Step four is the surfaces. Walls get repaired and painted in light, neutral colours, which make rooms feel larger and are easy to touch up between tenants. Floors should be durable. Tiles or good quality hard flooring handle wear much better than something delicate. The kitchen and bathroom matter most. You do not always need a full rebuild. Sometimes new doors on the existing kitchen cabinets, a new worktop and new taps will transform a kitchen. In the bathroom, fix leaks, re-seal, re-grout, or replace the sanitaryware if it is beyond saving. Tenants forgive plain. They do not forgive grubby or broken. Quality workmanship is what keeps a rental looking good between tenants.
