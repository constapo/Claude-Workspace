## Section 2: Step one, the roof (0:55-1:55)
On-screen text: "STEP 1: Roof. Reflective coating. Insulation. Waterproofing in good condition."
B-roll: Flat roof with dark, sun-heated surface; reflective white or light coating being applied; insulation boards being laid; solar heater and tanks on the roof.

> Step one is the roof. There are two main options. The first is a reflective, light-coloured coating, which reduces how much heat the surface absorbs. It can help, but results vary by product, so ask for the data sheet and be wary of dramatic promises. The second is thermal insulation, which slows heat moving through the roof into the rooms below. On existing flat roofs, insulation is usually combined with waterproofing work, so the layers must be designed together, otherwise you risk trapping moisture or damaging the waterproofing. If your waterproofing is already failing, fix that first. In an apartment block, the roof is often shared, so you will need the agreement of the other owners. That is why we plan insulation and waterproofing together, after a site visit.

## Section 3: Step two, shading (1:55-2:50)
On-screen text: "STEP 2: Shade the glass from OUTSIDE. Shutters. Awnings. Pergolas. Deciduous trees."
B-roll: External roller shutters closing; an awning over a south-facing window; a pergola with climbing plant; a tree shading a west wall; interior curtains for contrast.

> Step two is shading, and the key word is outside. Once sunlight has passed through the glass, the heat is already in the room. Curtains and blinds inside help with glare, but they do much less for heat than shading outside. External shutters, awnings, pergolas and louvres on south and west windows make a real difference. Trees help too, especially deciduous ones that give shade in summer and let light through in winter. Think about the sun's path. East windows get morning sun, west windows get hot afternoon sun, and a south-facing window can be shaded with a fixed overhang, because the summer sun is high.

## Section 4: Step three, windows and doors (3:10-4:10)
On-screen text: "STEP 3: Windows. Double glazing. Thermal break frames. Low-emissivity or solar control glass. Seals."
B-roll: Old single-glazed window with a gap; modern double-glazed unit cross-section (sample piece if you have one); worn rubber seals; a tradesperson installing a window.

> Step three is windows and doors. Single glazing and old frames let heat in and cool air out. Modern double glazing helps, and frames with a thermal break reduce heat transfer through the metal. Ask about glass with a low-emissivity or solar control coating, especially for large windows facing west or south. But replacing windows is expensive, so do the cheaper things first. Check the seals, repair gaps around frames, and fit shading outside. Replace windows when they are failing, leaking, or when you are renovating anyway. ## Section 5: Step four, walls, air leaks and ventilation (4:10-4:55)
On-screen text: "STEP 4: Walls and gaps. External wall insulation. Seal leaks. Night ventilation. Ceiling fans."
B-roll: External wall insulation being fixed to a wall (if available); window open at night with a gentle breeze; a ceiling fan; a tradesperson sealing a gap.

> Step four covers walls, gaps and ventilation. Insulating the outside of walls can reduce heat gain, and it also helps with winter damp, as we covered in an earlier video. But it is a bigger job, best planned with a specialist. Day to day, ventilation is free. If nights are cooler, open windows in the evening and early morning to flush out heat, then close up and shade the windows during the hottest hours. Ceiling fans help people feel cooler using little power. And check whether any government energy efficiency schemes are currently available, because conditions change.

- Narration uses "we" for Inonda. Everything else (on-screen text, B-roll notes, thumbnails, description, tags) is production material, not spoken.
- Under each narrated section is a "Greek subtitles (Ελληνικά)" block in SRT style: index, timecode range, then two lines of up to 42 characters. Numbering runs continuously through each video, so the blocks can be pasted in order into one .srt file.
- Section times and subtitle timecodes are calculated from the word count at about 140 words per minute plus a short pause between sections. Please re-time the section markers, the chapter list in the description and the subtitle timecodes to the final recorded voiceover.
- Anything in square brackets, like [ADD REAL PRICE, e.g. EUR X-Y], [ADD PHONE/WHATSAPP], [ADD WEBSITE] is a placeholder for the owner to fill with real details before recording. Do not read the brackets aloud. The Greek text uses matching Greek placeholders. No figures in these scripts are claims of fact, and no statistics, client names, awards or prices have been invented.
- Where contracts, deposits, guarantees, consumer rights or VAT come up, the scripts suggest checking with a Cyprus lawyer or accountant. Keep that wording in.
- On-screen text and B-roll notes are left in English. Translate the on-screen text if you also want a Greek-language version of the picture.
