Plain, light colours also photograph better and suit more people. Painting is part of what we do. The cost depends on size, so here is a placeholder: [ADD REAL PRICE, 2-bed flat].
>
> Upgrade two is fixing the small things. A dripping tap. A loose door handle. A cracked tile. A door that sticks. Silicone gone black around the bath or shower. Each one is cheap on its own. Together, they decide whether a viewer thinks, this is looked after, or, this is going to be trouble.
>
> Walk through your property with a notebook, as if you were the tenant, and write down every small annoyance. Then fix them in one visit. That is exactly the kind of job we are happy to quote for.

## Section 5: Photos, safety and what not to spend on (5:01-5:57)
> Now, two things that cost almost nothing. Good photos: wait for good daylight, tidy up, open the curtains, and photograph every room, plus the outdoor areas. Honest photos, no tricks.
>
> And basic safety: working smoke alarms, safe sockets and switches, a secure lock, and no obvious electrical problems. If you are unsure about the electrics or gas, have a qualified professional check them. That protects you, and it protects the tenant.
>
> What not to spend on? Be careful with expensive finishes that tenants will not pay extra for, or that will get damaged. Choose durable and easy to clean over luxurious. And do not spend a fortune before checking your local market. Look at similar listings in your area, and talk to a local letting agent.
