## 1:13-2:09 Mistake one: choosing on price alone

> Mistake number one is choosing on the lowest price alone. A very low quote can mean the contractor has missed things, or plans to cut corners, or intends to add extras later. It can also be perfectly fine, for example a smaller business with lower overheads. The point is not to avoid cheap quotes, but to understand them. Put your quotes side by side and ask why they differ. If one is far lower than the others, check what has been left out. Materials, waste removal, finishing, or a scope that is thinner than the others. At Inonda, we believe in honest quotes, so you can see exactly what is included and what is not. You are comparing like for like, or you are not comparing at all.

## 2:09-3:01 Mistake two: no site visit, and a vague scope

> Mistake number two is accepting a price without a site visit, and without a clear scope. If someone quotes over the phone, or from two photos, that is a guess. A proper visit means measurements, questions, and looking at the hidden risks like damp or old pipework. Local knowledge matters here, because homes in Cyprus can hide very different problems depending on their age and how they were built. Then insist the scope is written down. What will be done, what materials, what finish, and what is not included. A vague scope is where almost every dispute begins. If it is not written, it is not agreed, and then it becomes your word against theirs.

## 3:17-4:10 Mistake three: large upfront payments and no payment plan

> Mistake number three is paying too much upfront, with no clear payment plan. It is normal for a contractor to ask for a deposit to cover early costs, but how much is reasonable depends on the job. Rather than one big payment at the start, ask for payments linked to stages of work completed. Always pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for receipts. If a contractor insists on a large cash payment before any work begins, that is a warning sign. This protects both sides, and it is how we like to keep things clear with our own clients. For the legal side of deposits and contracts, please check with a Cyprus lawyer before you sign.

## 4:10-5:00 Mistake four: not checking the contractor

> Mistake number four is not checking who you are hiring. Ask for photos of recent similar work, and ask if you can speak to a past customer. Ask whether they are a registered business, and about insurance. Ask who will actually be on site, because sometimes the person who quotes is not the person who does the work. Look at online reviews with a sensible eye, and remember that a few reviews prove very little either way. A good contractor will not be offended by any of these questions. If they are, that tells you something. We are always happy to answer them, and we think that is how trust should start.
