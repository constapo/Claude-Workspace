## Section 2: Spring, March to May (1:09-2:15)
> Spring is the time to inspect what winter did, and prepare for summer.
>
> First, look for damp. After the wet months, check ceilings, corners, behind wardrobes, and around windows. If you find a stain, find the cause now, before painting over it. Damp treatment starts with understanding where the water is coming from.
>
> Second, get your air conditioning serviced before the first heatwave, not during it. Clean or replace filters, and check for water dripping and unusual noise.
>
> Third, check the roof and terrace. Look for cracks, lifted waterproofing, and blocked outlets. Spring is a good time for roofing and waterproofing repairs, because the weather is kind to the work.
>
> Fourth, outside. Garden irrigation, gates, shutters and mosquito screens. Fix them before they are needed. Also repaint or treat anything that is peeling.

## Section 3: Summer, June to August (2:15-3:17)
> Summer is about heat, and about not making problems worse.
>
> Keep air conditioning filters clean. A clogged filter works harder and cools less. Do not set it as cold as it will go. A moderate setting is kinder to the unit and to your electricity bill.
>
> Watch the water. Check the water tank and the float valve, look for any new damp patches, and keep an eye on the pool equipment if you have a pool. If you are away, make sure someone checks the house.
>
> Look at sun damage. Exterior sealant, window frames, shutters, wooden pergolas and terrace surfaces can dry and crack in strong sun. Treat small cracks quickly. Wooden items usually need re-treating on a regular basis.
>
> And keep shading in place. Awnings, shutters and curtains reduce heat before it enters the house, which helps comfort.

## Section 4: Autumn, September to November (3:40-4:55)
> Autumn is the most important season on this list, because it is your last chance before the rain.
>
> Start with the roof and terrace. Clear leaves, dust and debris from outlets and gutters, and check that water drains away from the building, not towards it. If waterproofing has cracked over summer, fix it now.
>
> Check windows and doors. Look at seals and frames. Any gap that lets in a draught will also let in water in a storm.
>
> Check the drains and the area around the house. Make sure rainwater has a clear path away. Clear gullies, and check boundary walls and steps.
>
> If you have a boiler, water heater, or any heating, get it checked before you need it. Test portable heaters and check cables. Heating should be serviced by a qualified person, especially if it uses gas or a flue.
>
> Finally, check the electrics visually. Look at outdoor lights, sockets and anything exposed. Damp and electricity do not mix, so if anything looks damaged, call a qualified electrician.

## Section 5: Winter, December to February (4:55-6:03)
> Winter is about watching, not fixing. Most of the work was done in autumn.
>
> After heavy rain, walk through the house. Look at ceilings, walls and windows for new stains, drips or dampness. Catching a leak on day one is far cheaper than finding it in spring.
>
> Fight condensation and mould. Open windows for a short time every day, even when it is cool, to let moist air out. Keep furniture slightly away from external walls. Use extractor fans in the kitchen and bathroom. If mould appears, clean it. But also find out why it is there, because cleaning alone will not stop it returning.
>
> Heat safely. Never block ventilation when using heaters, and never leave heaters unattended. Check smoke alarms and, if you use gas or any fuel heater, carbon monoxide alarms.
>
> And if the house is empty, arrange a visit after any big storm. A quick check can save a lot of money.
