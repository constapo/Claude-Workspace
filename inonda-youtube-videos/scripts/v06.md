## Section 3: The checklist, part one: water paths (1:15-2:20)
On-screen text: "1. Drains and outlets 2. Gutters and downpipes 3. Falls and ponding". Ticks appear as each item is spoken.
B-roll: Close-up of a clogged drain outlet; clearing leaves by hand with gloves; water poured from a bucket to test flow; a downpipe running to the ground; a puddle that stays after watering.

> Number one is drainage, because water that cannot leave will find another way in. Clear every roof drain, outlet and scupper of leaves, dust and debris. Check gutters and downpipes for blockages, loose brackets and cracks. Then pour a bucket of water across the surface and watch where it goes. It should flow towards the drains. If you see ponding, meaning puddles that stay for a day or more, the surface falls may not be right, and standing water ages waterproofing faster. A small puddle is not a disaster, but make a note and talk to a professional, because correcting the slope is usually a bigger job than patching. We are always honest about this: if a patch will do, we will say so.

## Section 4: The checklist, part two: the surface and joints (2:20-3:20)
On-screen text: "4. Waterproofing membrane 5. Edges, parapets, upstands 6. Penetrations 7. Tile joints and sealant".
B-roll: Blistered or cracked membrane close-up; corner where roof meets parapet wall with a gap; base of a solar water heater or aerial; cracked tile grout; sealant gun and fresh sealant bead.

> Number two is the waterproof layer. Look for cracks, blisters, splits, or areas where the coating is flaking or has gone chalky. Pay special attention to where leaks usually begin: the corner where the roof meets a parapet wall, the base of solar water heaters, tank stands and aerials, pipes that pass through the roof, and any expansion joints. Then check your terrace tiles. Missing grout, loose tiles and cracked sealant let water in underneath, where you cannot see it. Inside the house, look at ceilings and the tops of walls for stains, bubbling paint or a musty smell. And take photos now, so you can compare later. Clear photos also help us give you an accurate quote.

> There are three levels of repair. Small repairs include cleaning the drains, resealing around pipes, and replacing cracked sealant or grout. Medium repairs mean patching a damaged area of waterproofing. That must be done properly, with the surface clean, dry and primed according to the product instructions, and with overlaps at the edges. Large repairs mean re-waterproofing the whole roof or terrace, sometimes after fixing the falls or replacing damaged screed and tiles. Which one you need depends on the age and condition of the existing system, and that needs a site visit. Please be careful with quick fixes. Painting over a leak, or rolling a thin coat over damp or loose material, usually fails, often just when the rain arrives.
