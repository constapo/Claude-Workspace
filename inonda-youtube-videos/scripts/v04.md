## Step 1: Inspection and finding the source (1:00-1:55)
> Step one is the inspection, and it is detective work. Water travels. It can enter at one point and appear on a ceiling metres away. So we look at the inside and the outside together. On the roof or terrace we check for cracks, blistering, lifted edges, damaged joints around drains, gaps where walls meet the floor, and any spot where water sits instead of running away. We check the drains and outlets for blockages. We check parapet walls, pipes and vents that pass through the surface, because those are common weak points. Sometimes a water test helps, where we isolate an area and wet it systematically to see where the leak appears. And we take photos before anything is touched, so you can see exactly what we see.

## Step 2: Diagnosis and choosing the repair (1:55-2:40)
> Step two is the diagnosis. Not every leak needs a whole new roof. A single crack or failed joint might need a local repair. A tired, widespread surface might need a new waterproofing system across the whole area. Drainage problems need drainage fixes, otherwise the new surface will fail too. Ask the contractor to show you the problem and explain the reason in plain words. If the answer is just, we will put something over it, ask more questions. A good repair addresses the cause. We would always rather explain honestly and recommend the right fix than sell you a bigger job than you need.

## Step 3: Preparation of the surface (2:55-3:40)
> Step three is preparation, and this is where most repairs are won or lost. The old surface is cleaned. Loose, blistered or failed material is removed. Cracks are opened up and repaired. Weak areas are made good, and the surface is levelled where needed, so water runs towards the drains instead of sitting in puddles. Then it must be completely dry. Applying a waterproof coating over a damp surface traps moisture and leads to blisters and failure. This is not the exciting part, but it is the part that decides how long the repair lasts. It is also the part we never rush.

**On-screen text:** "3. Preparation: clean, repair, level, dry".

## Step 4: Waterproofing system (3:40-4:30)
> Step four is the waterproofing itself. Depending on the surface, this might be a liquid-applied membrane, a bitumen or similar sheet membrane, or another system suited to the building. Whatever is chosen, detail is everything. Corners, edges, drain outlets, pipes and the junction between the floor and the walls need extra attention, usually with reinforcement. The membrane is typically carried up the walls to a suitable height and finished properly. Several coats or layers are applied according to the manufacturer's instructions, with the correct drying time between each. Ask which product is used, and check that it is suitable for sun exposure, or that it will be protected by a finishing layer.

**On-screen text:** "4. Waterproofing: details decide success".

## Step 5: Protection, finishing and the water test (4:30-5:10)
> Step five is protection and testing. On a terrace, the membrane may be covered with screed and tiles. On a roof, it may get a reflective or protective coat to reduce heat and ageing. Then comes the part we like most. We test it. We block the outlets, flood the area to a safe depth for a period, and check the ceiling below. If it stays dry, we know the repair works. Then we clear the water, unblock the drains and finish the edges. Please ask your contractor whether they do a water test. It is the proof.
