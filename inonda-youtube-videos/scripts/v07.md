## Section 2: Priority one, safety and structure (0:55-2:05)
On-screen text: "PRIORITY 1: Safety and structure. Railings. Balcony concrete. Anything people lean on or stand under."
B-roll: Rusty railing base where it meets the concrete; balcony soffit with rust stains; concrete spalling with exposed steel; a hand tapping gently on concrete. Do not show anyone leaning on a damaged railing.

> Priority one is anything that affects safety. Start with balcony and stair railings. Check the base, where the post meets the concrete, because rust often hides there. If a railing moves, wobbles or has corroded through, treat it as urgent and keep people away from it. Next, look at balcony and terrace concrete, especially the underside. Rust-brown stains, cracks following a line, or pieces of concrete falling away to expose steel bars, all mean the steel inside is corroding. Rust takes up more space than the original steel, so it pushes the concrete apart, and the damage speeds up. This is not a painting job. The loose concrete must be removed, the steel treated or replaced, and the concrete repaired properly. If you see exposed or swollen steel, ask a structural engineer or an experienced specialist to assess it before any work begins.

## Section 3: Priority two, keeping water out (2:05-2:55)
On-screen text: "PRIORITY 2: Water ingress. Window and door seals. Roof and terrace waterproofing. Cracked render."
B-roll: Cracked sealant around an aluminium window; salt-crusted door threshold; cracked render with rust bleed; terrace edge.

> Priority two is keeping water out. Salt can make sealants harden and crack faster, and worn seals around windows, doors and terrace edges let wind-driven rain into the walls. Cracked render does the same. Once water is inside, it feeds damp and corrosion behind the surface, where you cannot see it. So check the sealants around frames, door thresholds and terrace edges, and look for render cracks. Repair them before the rainy season. If your roof or terrace waterproofing is old, have it inspected too. This is everyday work for us: sealing, render repair and waterproofing, done carefully.

## Section 4: Priority three, metalwork, fixings and equipment (3:15-4:20)
On-screen text: "PRIORITY 3: Metal and equipment. Aluminium frames. Gates and railings. Air conditioner units. Solar heaters. Outdoor electrics."
B-roll: Pitted aluminium frame with white powdery corrosion; rusty gate hinges; air conditioner outdoor unit with corroded fins; a solar water heater frame; outdoor socket or light fitting with corrosion.

> Priority three is metalwork and equipment. Aluminium frames can show white, powdery corrosion and pitting, especially if the coating is damaged. Light surface corrosion can be cleaned and protected, but if a frame is deeply pitted or the hardware fails, replacement may be better value. Iron gates and railings should have rust removed back to sound metal, then be primed and painted with a system suitable for marine conditions. When replacing fixings, ask for stainless steel suited to coastal use, and avoid mixing different metals where they touch. Outdoor air conditioner units and solar heaters suffer too, so ask the installer about coastal protection and cleaning. And if you see corrosion on electrical parts, have a qualified electrician look at it.

## Section 5: Priority four, coatings and the routine that saves money (4:20-5:10)
On-screen text: "PRIORITY 4: Protection and appearance. Exterior paint. Timber. Regular rinse with fresh water."
B-roll: A painter preparing a surface; a hose rinsing a window and railing; a wooden pergola with peeling varnish; a before and after of a repainted railing.

> Priority four is coatings and appearance. Fresh paint is not just cosmetic. It is a protective layer, but it only lasts if the surface is prepared properly. Painting over loose rust or salt-covered surfaces fails quickly. So wash the salt off with fresh water, remove loose material, and use products designed for exterior and coastal conditions. Timber, such as pergolas and shutters, needs a good exterior treatment and regular care. And here is the cheapest habit of all. Rinse windows, railings and outdoor equipment with fresh water from time to time, gently, not with a high-pressure jet that could damage seals. Add a short inspection once or twice a year, and small problems stay small.
