## 0:55-1:50 Repairs 1 to 3
**Narration (English):**

> Number one: re-siliconing a shower or sink edge. In humid bathrooms, silicone goes black or peels. Cut out the old bead, clean the area, and let it dry fully. Apply a fresh line with masking tape either side, smooth it, peel the tape, and let it cure before using the shower. Number two: grout touch-up. Scrape out loose or stained grout, clean the gap, press in fresh grout, and wipe the tile face before it hardens. Number three: a door that sticks. Find where it rubs. Often a loose hinge screw is the cause, so tighten them first. In Cyprus, wood swells in humid weather, so if it still rubs, mark the spot, sand or plane it lightly, and seal the bare edge with paint or varnish.

## 1:50-2:45 Repairs 4 to 6
**Narration (English):**

> Number four: a dripping tap. Turn off the water at the isolation valve, open the tap to release pressure, and see what type of tap you have. Often the cause is a worn washer or cartridge, an inexpensive part. Take the old one to a shop to match it. Number five: a squeaky or loose hinge. Tighten the screws. If the hole is stripped, push in a matchstick or a small dowel with glue, then re-drive the screw, and add a little lubricant. Number six: cabinet doors and handles. Many kitchen doors hang badly because the adjustment screws have shifted. Small turns on the hinge screws usually bring them back into line. A tidy kitchen often starts with small things like this.

**Narration (English):**

> Number nine: a toilet cistern that keeps running. Turn off the water to the cistern, remove the lid, and look at the float and the flush seal. Anything beyond that belongs to a qualified air conditioning technician.

## 4:30-5:30 When to call a professional
**Narration (English):**

> So when should you stop and call someone? If a leak keeps coming back, or damp is spreading on a wall or ceiling, call a plumber or a repair specialist, because the visible leak is often not the real source. Damp treatment and waterproofing are exactly the kind of work we do, and we look for the cause, not just paint over the stain. If cracks are wide, growing, or running diagonally across walls, treat them as potentially structural and get them assessed properly. If a socket is warm, a switch crackles, a breaker keeps tripping, or you smell burning, switch off what you safely can and call a licensed electrician. If you smell gas, don't touch switches. Open windows and contact your gas supplier or the emergency services. And if a job is bigger than your tools, time or confidence, call someone. That isn't failure. It's good judgement.
