## 1:28-2:38 Driver one: size, layout and whether the plumbing moves

> Driver number one is layout. Size matters, but not as much as you might think. A small bathroom with a new shower, toilet and basin in exactly the same positions as before is a very different job from a bathroom where you want the toilet moved to the other wall. The moment pipes and drainage have to move, you are paying for more plumbing, more breaking of floors and walls, and more making good afterwards. Changing a bath into a walk-in shower is another common request. It looks simple in photos, but the floor often needs to be reworked so that water drains properly. This is where local knowledge helps. We know how homes in Cyprus are typically built, and what is often waiting under the floor. So before you fall in love with a design, ask yourself one gentle question. Can everything stay where it is? If the answer is yes, you have just protected your budget.

## 2:38-3:50 Driver two: what is behind the walls

> Driver number two is the one nobody enjoys talking about, and that is what is hidden. Once the old tiles come off, we sometimes find tired old pipes, damp that has been quietly spreading, or a floor that was never properly waterproofed. In many Cyprus homes, especially older ones, bathrooms were done to a very different standard decades ago. None of that is visible on the day someone visits to quote. So a good contractor will tell you this honestly, and will explain how any extra work will be priced and agreed before it happens. That is how we like to work at Inonda. Clear communication, and no unexplained extras. Waterproofing and damp treatment are the areas where we would never cut corners. A leak behind a finished wall costs far more to fix than doing it right the first time. So when you compare quotes, ask each person one question. What happens if you find a problem once the walls are open?

## 4:12-5:18 Driver three: materials, fittings and finish

> Driver number three is the part everyone thinks of first. Materials and fittings. Tiles, sanitaryware, taps, shower screens, mirrors and lighting can range from simple and practical to very high-end, and that is where your taste sets the budget. But there are two details people often miss. First, large-format tiles and complex patterns take longer and need more skill to lay. So labour rises, even if the tile itself is not expensive. Second, cheap fittings can cost more over time, if spare parts are hard to find or they fail early. Our advice is to decide where you want to spend. Perhaps a quality shower mixer and a good screen, with simple tiles elsewhere. Write down what you want before you ask for quotes, so everyone is pricing the same thing. And if you are not sure, we are always happy to talk through your options with you.

## 5:18-6:18 Driver four: the practical side, access, trades and timing

> Driver number four is practical. Think about access. An apartment on the fourth floor without a lift is harder work than a ground-floor house, because rubble and materials have to be carried out. Check with your building management about working hours and rules for shared areas. Then think about trades. A bathroom usually needs a plumber, an electrician, a tiler and sometimes a painter, and someone has to coordinate them. If nobody does, you get delays, and delays cost money. A good contractor takes that coordination off your shoulders, and that is the standard we hold ourselves to. Some works may also need permission or notification, so ask your contractor, and if you are unsure, speak to a Cyprus lawyer or the relevant authority. Finally, remember that rubble removal and clean-up should be written into the quote.
