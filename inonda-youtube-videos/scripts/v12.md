## 1:19-2:12 What a good quote includes: the basics

> Let us start with the basics. A good quote has the contractor's full business details, your name and the address of the property, and the date. It should say how long the quote is valid, because material prices can change. Then it should describe the work. Not just bathroom renovation, but what exactly. Removing old tiles, new waterproofing, new plumbing points, tiling, fitting sanitaryware, painting. Think of the description as a shopping list of tasks. If a job is on your wish list but not in the quote, assume it is not included until it is confirmed in writing. At Inonda, we write our quotes this way on purpose, because clear communication at the start saves a lot of worry later.

## 2:12-3:16 Materials, labour and allowances

> Next, materials and labour. A clear quote shows what is supplied by whom. Who buys the tiles, you or the contractor? Which brand or grade of materials? If the quote says allowance, such as an allowance for tiles, understand that this is a budget figure, and the real cost could be higher or lower depending on what you choose. That is fine, as long as you know it. Labour should be described by task or stage, so you can compare. Ask whether the price is fixed for the described scope, or an estimate that can change. And ask about VAT. Is it included, and at what rate? If you are not sure how it applies to your job, ask your accountant or the relevant authority. Honest quotes mean no hidden lines and no awkward surprises, and that is the standard we aim for.

## 4:30-5:23 Red flags

> Now the red flags. One, a single total with no breakdown. Two, a vague description, with phrases like all necessary works and nothing specific. Three, pressure to sign today because the price will rise tomorrow. A genuine offer can survive a night's sleep. Four, a request for a large payment upfront, or for cash only with no receipt. Five, no business details, no address, and no way to follow up later. Six, a price far lower than every other quote, with no explanation. And seven, a contractor who gets annoyed when you ask questions. None of these proves someone is dishonest, but each one is a reason to slow down and ask more. Trust your instincts, and take your time.
