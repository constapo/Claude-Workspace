If the layout truly does not work, then it is a bigger project. But try the simple route first. When we visit a kitchen, this is always our starting point, because the best saving is the thing you do not have to replace.

## Step 2: Where to save (1:55-2:45)
> Step two is where to save. New doors and drawer fronts on existing cabinets can change the whole look. Painting or wrapping is cheaper again, if the surface is sound. New handles cost little, but change the feel completely. A new splashback, or even just re-sealing and cleaning the old one, makes a visible difference. Open shelving in one area can replace a wall unit. And a plain, light colour scheme looks clean and modern, and it will not date quickly. Be careful with very cheap materials that swell when wet. In a Cyprus summer, with humidity, heat and cooking, poor materials fail quickly. Saving should never mean cutting corners on quality.

## Step 4: The order of work (3:50-4:35)
> Step four is the order of work, which keeps costs down and avoids rework. First, strip out what is going. Second, any plumbing and electrical changes. Third, wall repairs and painting. Fourth, flooring, if it is being replaced. Fifth, cabinets, doors and worktops. Sixth, splashback, taps and appliances. Last, the final sealing, cleaning and adjustments. Painting before the worktop goes in, for example, saves you protecting a brand new surface. Measure twice, order once, and confirm delivery dates before you start, because waiting for one missing part can hold up the whole job. Planning the order well is one of the simplest ways we help owners save money.
